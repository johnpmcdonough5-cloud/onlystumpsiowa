Will a Ground Stump Grow Back?

August 13, 2026 · OnlyStumps

A ground stump almost never grows back on its own. What does come back are suckers off the root system, and a handful of Iowa tree species are notorious for it.

The stump itself is done

Once we grind a stump at least 6 inches below grade, the trunk tissue that could push new growth is gone. There is no bud and no cambium layer left above ground to sprout from. That part is finished.

What survives is the root system, and roots are the whole story here. Some species treat a cut trunk as a signal to send up shoots from those roots, sometimes several feet from where the tree actually stood.

The Iowa species that sucker

Cottonwood, poplar, silver maple, black locust, tree of heaven, and box elder are the usual offenders around Cedar Rapids and Waterloo. Willow and elm do it too. If you had one of those, expect some shoots.

Oak, ash, walnut, and most conifers rarely sucker at all. Grind one of those and you are typically done with it the same day.

What suckers look like

You will see thin green shoots coming straight up out of the lawn in a rough ring around where the stump was, usually within a few weeks during growing season. They look like weeds until you notice the leaves match the tree you just removed.

They are not deep-rooted and they are not dangerous. They are persistent, and mowing over them without dealing with the root will keep them coming back all season.

How to stop them

Mowing works if you stay on it. Every time you cut a shoot down, the root spends stored energy replacing it. Keep cutting through one full season and most root systems give up.

For a faster end, cut the shoots and treat the fresh cut with a brush killer containing triclopyr within a few minutes, while the cut is still taking up fluid. Follow the label, and keep it off anything you want to keep.

The other option is taking out more root mass up front. If the tree was a known suckering species, tell us before we grind and we can remove more of the lateral roots while the machine is already on site.

What this means if you are reseeding

If you plan to seed the spot this fall, deal with suckers first. New grass and tree shoots compete for the same water and light, and the shoots win.

The timing works in your favor. Late August into mid-September is the strongest seeding window in eastern Iowa, and it lines up with a season of mowing suckers down. Grind now, keep the shoots cut, and seed into a clean spot.

Common questions

How long do suckers keep coming back?

One growing season of consistent mowing kills off most root systems. Aggressive species like cottonwood and black locust can take two.

Does grinding deeper stop suckering?

It helps but does not guarantee it. Suckers come off lateral roots that can run well past the stump, so depth alone does not reach all of them.

Can you kill the roots chemically instead of grinding?

You can, but it is slow and the stump still sits there rotting for years. Grinding is faster and leaves you a spot you can actually plant.

Will the leftover roots hurt my new grass?

No. Buried roots rot on their own over a year or two. The only thing to plan for is the ground settling a little as they break down.
